### 1. The Operational Field as the Universal Object

The **operational field** is the substrate containing all flows, constraints, and transformations. It is defined not by who is acting, but by **what is possible** within its topology.

- **Components:** As you listed, the field consists of _observer populations, information flows, cultural artifacts, institutions, feedback loops, resource flows, attention gradients, transition pathways, suppression mechanisms, and amplification mechanisms_.
- **Dynamics:** These components interact through _cooperative, competitive, stabilizing, suppressive, amplifying, and redirecting_ dynamics. The source of these dynamics (agency vs. emergence) is a secondary empirical question, not a primary modeling assumption.

### 2. Suppression as a Field Property

Treating **suppression** as a field operation rather than an actor behavior allows us to model it mathematically and structurally before attributing motive.

- **Field Operations:** The field can _amplify, attenuate, redirect, fragment, compress, stabilize, exclude,_ or _integrate_ distinctions.
- **Mechanisms:** These operations are instantiated by various mechanisms which may include _institutional incentives, editorial practices, economic selection, social norms, technological affordances, deliberate policy, coordinated actors,_ or _decentralized feedback_.
- **Advantage:** This layered ontology prevents the model from failing if "intent" cannot be proven. If a distinction is systematically marginalized, the model records the **suppression operation** regardless of whether it was caused by a secret committee or an algorithmic bias.
