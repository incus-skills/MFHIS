## Xenophon’s Oeconomicus, oikonomia (οἰκονομία)

---

The **city-state economy** (specifically the Greek _polis_) was established through a structural evolution from independent household units (_oikos_) into a collective political and economic entity, driven by geographic necessity, trade expansion, and institutional innovation.

### 1. Aggregation of Households into the Polis

The economy originated in the **Archaic Period (c. 800–500 BCE)** when scattered villages and noble estates unified to form self-governing city-states. Unlike the Mycenaean palace economies which relied on centralized redistribution, the _polis_ emerged as a network of autonomous **households (_oikos_)**.

- **Foundation**: The _polis_ was viewed as a natural extension of the household; while the _oikos_ managed daily survival and biological needs, the _polis_ was created to secure the "good life" and self-sufficiency (_autarky_) that individual households could not achieve alone.
- **Land Ownership**: Economic power was initially tied to land. Only citizens could own land, creating an oligarchic structure where the elite controlled agricultural production, the primary economic engine.

### 2. Geographic Drivers and Trade Specialization

The mountainous terrain of Greece limited arable land, forcing city-states to abandon pure self-sufficiency in favor of **specialization and maritime trade**.

- **Resource Scarcity**: Most poleis could not produce enough grain to support their populations. This necessitated a shift from subsistence farming to producing high-value cash crops like **olives, wine, and pottery** for export.
- **Maritime Networks**: Cities like **Athens** and **Corinth** leveraged their access to the sea to import grain (from Egypt and the Black Sea) and raw materials (timber, metals) in exchange for manufactured goods. This transformed the economy from a closed household system to an open, market-oriented network.

### 3. Institutional Innovations: The Agora and Coinage

The physical and financial infrastructure of the city-state economy centered on two key innovations that distinguished it from household management:

- **The Agora**: This central marketplace served as the economic heart of the _polis_, replacing the private threshold of the _oikos_. It was a public space where merchants, farmers, and craftsmen engaged in price negotiations, fostering competition and specialization. Crucially, it also functioned as a civic hub where economic activity intersected with political debate.
- **Coinage**: The introduction of standardized currency (first in Lydia, quickly adopted by Greek poleis) facilitated complex trade beyond simple barter. Each city-state often minted its own coins, which acted as both a medium of exchange and a symbol of political sovereignty and economic power.

### 4. Social Stratification and Civic Finance

As trade enriched a new class of merchants and artisans, the economic balance shifted away from the landed aristocracy.

- **Rise of the Middle Class**: Wealth generated from commerce allowed non-nobles to demand political rights, leading to reforms (such as those by tyrants or democratic leaders) that integrated economic contribution with civic participation.
- **Liturgies**: To fund public infrastructure and military needs without a modern tax bureaucracy, the _polis_ institutionalized **liturgies** (_leitourgiai_). Wealthy citizens were legally required to finance public goods (e.g., equipping a warship or funding a chorus), effectively redirecting private household wealth into the public economy to ensure the state's stability.

$$\text{Contribution Weight } (W_c) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \text{Base Work Input} \times \text{Dependency Depth} \times \text{Usage Frequency} \right)$$

[ecb.europa](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp2630~94d9b91ed7.en.pdf)

## What this means structurally

The compensation model then has two layers:

- **Immediate liquidity** for daily living.
- **Deferred productive yield** as long-horizon security.

That deferred yield is what replaces the usual pension logic. Instead of saving in a currency that can be diluted externally, members accumulate claims on:

- land usufruct,
- treasury assets,
- internal ventures,
- and productive infrastructure.

That makes the community’s “retirement” logic a continuation of stewardship, not passive cash hoarding. [pensionspolicyinstitute.org](https://www.pensionspolicyinstitute.org.uk/media/c00dra0k/20240909-ppi-pension-scheme-assets-main-report-final.pdf)

## Why it works

If the community is converting external earnings into hard assets and productive capacity, then internal remuneration is effectively priced against a stronger base than the outside fiat system. That means:

- workers can be paid in a unit that tracks real productivity,
- the community can preserve value across time,
- and the yield from communal assets can function like a social annuity. [frbsf](https://www.frbsf.org/wp-content/uploads/Corsetti_CPBS_2020.pdf)

## Practical framing

So the internal economy is not just “higher pay.” It is:

- **higher real compensation** relative to the debased external unit,
- plus **asset-backed future claims** that act as savings,
- plus **periodic reset logic** that prevents wealth lock-in.

## Best concise formulation

> If the internal economy is insulated from external inflation, then relative internal compensation rises, and the yield from community-held productive assets becomes the functional equivalent of savings and pension wealth.

That is the correct direction for the model.
