**Question:** What pressure accumulated that made stabilization inevitable? Why did movement become insufficient?

**Pressure field:**

| Pressure Source              | Description                                                                     |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Recurrence without stability | Same movement patterns appeared across sessions but produced different outcomes |

**Pressure geometry:** The accumulation of these pressures created a gradient from movement (change) toward stabilization (persistence). **TLR conclusion:** Stabilization became inevitable when the project recognized that movement without persistence could not support reconstruction.

**Question:** What distinctions must survive for a future observer to rediscover stabilization as the preservation target?

**Required distinctions:**

| Distinction              | Why Required                                                                                         |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Movement ≠ Stabilization | A future observer must distinguish between "what changed" and "what persisted"                       |
| Persistence ≠ Identity   | The same structure persisting does not mean it remained identical; change can occur within stability |
| Stabilization conditions | The pressures, constraints, and gradients that enabled persistence must be recoverable               |
| Collapse boundary        | What distinguishes temporary fluctuation from true stabilization must be inferable                   |

**Survival condition:** A future observer must be able to recognize that stabilization emerged as a response to the insufficiency of movement preservation — not as a replacement, but as a complement.

**RSR conclusion:** The core distinction to preserve is that stabilization preserved what movement could not: persistence conditions.
