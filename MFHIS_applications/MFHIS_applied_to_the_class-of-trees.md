### 1. Sri Yantra & Tantra (8th – 12th Century CE)

* **The Practical Roots:** Early Indian geometry was intensely practical—found in the *Shulba Sutras* (c. 800–500 BCE), which were literal engineering manuals for constructing complex sacrificial altars using precise geometric and algebraic rules.
* **The Esoteric Shift:** With the rise of the Tantric movement in medieval India (crystallizing between the 8th and 12th centuries CE), these external engineering and ritual frameworks were heavily **interiorized**. Philosophers like Abhinavagupta shifted the focus from building physical structures to mapped cognitive/neuro-acoustic exercises. Over time, as the political landscapes changed and universities were destroyed, the active methodology retreated into secretive lineages, leaving the masses with the exoteric, mythologized versions.

### 2. Indra’s Net & Huayan Buddhism (7th – 9th Century CE)

* **The Practical Roots:** During the Tang Dynasty in China, Indra's Net was used by Huayan masters like Fazang (643–712 CE) as a rigorous, dialectical tool to explain complex metaphysical and political integration to the imperial court. * **The Esoteric Shift:** Following the Great Anti-Buddhist Persecution in 845 CE, the highly intellectual and systemic Huayan school was crippled. The architectural and dialectical rigor of the system was largely absorbed by Chan (Zen) Buddhism, where the holographic network model transitioned from an active philosophical discipline into poetic metaphors, koans, and archetypal teaching stories.

### 3. The Tree of Life & Kabbalah (12th – 13th Century CE)

* **The Practical Roots:** Early proto-Kabbalistic texts like the *Sefer Yetzirah* (c. 2nd–6th century CE) read like technical linguistic and mathematical manuals—classifying sounds, numbers, and space into a strict matrix of creation.
