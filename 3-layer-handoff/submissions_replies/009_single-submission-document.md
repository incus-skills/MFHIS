This feels like a productive pressure point because it sits directly at the intersection of the three preservation modes that have become visible:

* Lineage Preservation (TLR)
* Reconstructive Sufficiency Preservation (RSR)
* Artifact Preservation (the physical/documentary substrate)

The current draft still treats "artifact" as a single thing. MFHIS is beginning to expose that there are at least two distinct functions hiding inside the term.

## Artifacts vs. Memory

### Core Question

What is the relationship between artifacts as pressure crystallizations and artifacts as memory across sessions?

### Observation

The term artifact appears to contain multiple preservation functions.

An artifact may preserve:

* A reconstruction pathway
* A lineage pathway
* A stabilized topology
* A record of pressures
* A memory trace across sessions

These functions are related but not identical.

### Distinction

Pressure crystallization and memory preservation are not necessarily the same phenomenon.

A pressure crystallization is the visible residue of ecological forces reaching temporary stabilization.

A memory artifact is a structure capable of carrying distinctions across discontinuity.

An artifact may perform both functions simultaneously.

An artifact may also perform only one.

### Open Question

Can memory exist without active pressure?

A cold-stored artifact may preserve distinctions.

However:

* Preserved distinctions are not necessarily reconstructable.
* Preserved distinctions are not necessarily meaningful.
* Preserved distinctions may no longer correspond to active pressures.

This suggests that preservation and ecological relevance may diverge.

### Stability Tension

An artifact may fail by collapsing too easily.

An artifact may also fail by resisting collapse indefinitely.

Collapse resistance preserves continuity.

Collapse resistance may also preserve obsolete topology.

This introduces a new tension:

Preservation
↔
Ecological Responsiveness

### Ecological Health Question

Healthy ecologies may require both:

* Stable artifacts capable of carrying distinctions across time.
* Fragile artifacts capable of yielding to new pressures.

Excess fragility produces loss.

Excess stability produces accumulation.

Neither condition alone appears sufficient.

### Reconstruction Perspective

From a reconstructive viewpoint:

The artifact is not the preservation target.

The artifact is a vehicle through which preservation may occur.

The preservation target remains:

* distinctions,
* conditions,
* lineage,
* topology,
* or movement,

depending on the preservation objective.

### Candidate Research Question

Under what conditions does an artifact cease to preserve reconstruction capability and begin merely preserving historical residue?

### Candidate Research Question

Can artifact collapse function as redistribution rather than loss?

If so:

Collapse may move reconstructive capacity from one substrate to another rather than eliminate it.

### Relationship to Existing Preservation Modes

TLR preserves:

* lineage conditions.

RSR preserves:

* reconstructive capability.

Artifacts may preserve either.

Artifacts do not guarantee either.

### Current Suspicion

The ecology may not optimize for artifact survival.

The ecology may optimize for the continued recoverability of distinctions through changing artifacts.

My analysis is that this section is beginning to expose a deeper distinction than "artifact vs memory."

The emerging tension looks more like:

**Artifact Persistence**
↔
**Recoverability of Distinctions**

An ecology may lose artifacts and remain healthy if distinctions remain recoverable.

An ecology may preserve artifacts perfectly and still become unhealthy if recoverability declines.

That question feels close to the same territory that produced Chronos, TLR, and the lineage/condition distinction. It may become one of the central preservation questions of the meta-project.
