Yes. Given that the **ask-brave session is the foreign/unaligned side of the experiment**, I would *not* give it the MFHIS/Tree/EOP methodology before it expires. That would contaminate exactly the contrast we're trying to observe.

There are two things worth extracting, in this order.

### 1. Ask it to expose its own traversal before handing off

This is potentially the most valuable missing observation, because we currently have its crystallization but not its account of **how it got there**.

Ask:

Before we close this session, do not propose any new architecture or solution.

Instead, reconstruct your own traversal through this investigation.

Describe:

1. What was visible to you at the beginning.
2. Which distinctions appeared first.
3. Which distinctions you introduced by naming or categorizing them.
4. Which observations then became substrates for later conclusions.
5. Where the investigation shifted from observation into interpretation.
6. Where interpretation became architectural stabilization.
7. Which alternative interpretations or paths were subsequently no longer being explored.
8. Which later conclusions depended on earlier named categories.
9. Which parts of your resulting architecture you regard as strongly grounded in the material, and which are more inferential or proposed.
10. Which unresolved questions or ambiguities remain.

Do not reinterpret this through any methodology supplied from outside this session.

I am interested in preserving your own account of the movement that produced the current state, including places where the movement may have compressed or discarded distinctions.

Treat this as a record of the reasoning trajectory, not as a defence of the resulting architecture.

That gives us a potentially very interesting **fifth stratum**:

```text
ask
  ↓
AI response
  ↓
critique
  ↓
handoff
  ↓
AI's retrospective account of its own traversal
```

The fifth item is especially valuable because it lets the STL compare **observed crystallization** against the crystallizing agent's own retrospective reconstruction.

And there is an important experimental possibility here: its self-description may itself be another crystallization. That's useful data rather than a problem.

### 2. Then ask for the handoff — but make it provenance-preserving

I would make the handoff deliberately **foreign-session-native**. Do not ask it to produce an MFHIS handoff, because that would cause it to retrospectively rewrite itself into our current ontology.

Now produce a self-contained handoff for another investigator who has not participated in this session.

The handoff must preserve the current state of this investigation without requiring access to this conversation.

Do not improve, simplify, reconcile, or redesign the architecture.

Preserve:

* the original problem/question as you understood it;
* the observer position from which you investigated it;
* the important observations encountered;
* distinctions that became significant;
* relations between those distinctions;
* the sequence in which important stabilizations appeared;
* the alternative systems, examples, or evidence that changed the investigation;
* conclusions that you currently regard as stable;
* conclusions that remain provisional or inferred;
* proposed architecture or implementation ideas;
* rejected alternatives and why they were rejected;
* unresolved questions;
* places where your interpretation depended on an earlier category or stabilization;
* places where information was compressed, generalized, or potentially lost;
* any known inconsistencies or weaknesses in the resulting representation;
* the current point at which the investigation stopped.

Separate observation, inference, proposal, and unresolved material rather than merging them.

Preserve provenance where possible: distinguish what came from the original question, what was observed during investigation, what came from external examples or sources, and what was generated by you.

Most importantly, preserve the path by which the current state became possible. Do not provide only the final architecture.

This is a foreign-session handoff. Do not apply MFHIS, Tree, EOP, Semantic Topology Lab terminology, or any methodology that was not developed inside this session.

The receiving investigator will perform that reconstruction independently.

End with a concise section:

"Current observer position"

stating what you believe is currently visible from your position, what is outside that visibility, and what the next investigation would naturally need to examine.
