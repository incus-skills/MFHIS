# MFHIS Glossary (Current State)

Based on the project's documentation and usage throughout our conversation, **MFHIS** stands for:

**M**embrane **F**ield **H**omeomorphic **I**nvariant **S**emantics

But this is interpretive and in fact the acronym has stayed as a handle long after the expansion has been left behind, and likely doesn't fit the current usage.

mfhis -> mephis

This is the theoretical/explanatory layer of the Semantic Topology Lab—a developing language for describing how coherent separation and collapse interact across recursive systems.

The name reflects its core concerns:

- **Membrane** - selective permeability between epistemic domains
- **Field** - continuous gradients, not discrete entities
- **Homeomorphic** - topology-preserving transformation
- **Invariant** - what survives under transformation
- **Semantics** - meaning-bearing structure

MFHIS is not a completed theory. It is a progressively stabilizing reconstruction handle attached to an expanding field of observations about how coherent separation can be maintained under recursive compression and collapse pressure.

The project maintains an asymmetry: MFHIS may illuminate observations, but observations remain capable of challenging MFHIS.

## Core MFHIS Vocabulary

### Observation

The act of permitting distinctions to remain visible before explanation.

Observation precedes interpretation.

---

### Observer Position

The position from which distinctions become visible.

Different positions reveal different topologies.

Position is part of the observation.

---

### Distinction

A separation observed within terrain.

The fundamental unit of observation.

Distinctions precede classification.

---

### Relation

An observed connection between distinctions.

Relations are observed before being named.

---

### Gradient

A directional tendency revealed through relations.

Gradients indicate movement, pressure, tendency, or change.

---

### Topology

The arrangement revealed through gradients.

Not the terrain itself.

A visible organizational structure.

---

### Pressure

A force acting upon topology.

Pressure may reveal or conceal structure.

---

### Collapse Pressure

A pressure capable of flattening distinctions, gradients, or reconstructability.

Collapse is treated as degree rather than absolute disappearance.

---

### Reconstruction

The re-establishment of gradients sufficient for topology to become visible again.

Not replay.

Not duplication.

A reorientation process.

---

### Reorientation

A change in observer position resulting from reconstruction.

Reorientation permits new distinctions to become visible.

---

### Preservation

The maintenance of distinctions required for future reconstruction.

---

### Reconstructability

The ability for topology to become visible again through reconstruction.

The primary preservation criterion.

---

## Secondary MFHIS Vocabulary

### Compliance

The degree to which a topology yields to applied pressure.

Neither positive nor negative.

A property of the observed ecology.

---

### Stabilization

A configuration where gradients remain sufficiently established for topology to persist.

A condition, not a destination.

---

### Stabilization Path

The observed path through which stabilization became possible.

Distinct from the stabilized state itself.

---

### Ecology

The collection of forces, pressures, positions, distinctions, and interactions that generate observable topology.

Usually inferred rather than directly observed.

---

### Terrain

Whatever is being observed.

Topology emerges from terrain but is not identical to terrain.

---

### Visibility

The degree to which distinctions can be observed.

Visibility changes with position.

---

### Observational Position

The stabilized observer position through which topology becomes visible.

---

### Deferred Materialization

The tendency to continue observing after sufficient topology has already emerged.

A recurring collapse pressure.

---

### External Anchoring

Return to practical preservation requirements.

Examples include:

- Session loss
- Context loss
- Hardware failure
- Transfer requirements

---

## Morphological Vocabulary

### Locus

A recurring pattern of tensions, pressures, gradients, or failures.

Observed across multiple contexts.

---

### Morphology

The study of recurring structures across observations.

Concerned with recurrence rather than singular events.

---

### Anti-Locus (Provisional)

A recurring collapse configuration.

Not yet fully stabilized as a formal term.

---

## Preservation Vocabulary

### AOR

After-Observation Record.

Preserves emergence pathways.

Documents what became visible and how.

---

### TLR

Trace Lineage Record.

Preserves lineage conditions.

Documents antecedents, pressures, tensions, mutations, reorientations, and stabilization conditions that contributed to the emergence of a structure.

Answers:

"What made this possible?"

"What conditions preceded this stabilization?"

"What pressures were active before this became visible?"

Preservation target:

Lineage.

Risk:

Lineage preservation does not guarantee future reconstruction.

---

### RSR

Reconstructive Sufficiency Record.

Preserves conditions for reconstruction.

Documents distinctions, relationships, gradients, positions, dependencies, and preservation requirements necessary for a future observer to recover the topology.

Answers:

"What must remain available for this to be reconstructed?"

"What distinctions are necessary for future recovery?"

"What conditions must remain true?"

Preservation target:

Reconstructability.

Risk:

Reconstructive sufficiency does not guarantee preservation of lineage.

---

### RSS

Recursive Specification Stabilization.

Preserves the ecology that allows specifications to emerge, stabilize, and remain reconstructable.

Documents the recursive interaction between observation, pressure, distinction, preservation, stabilization, and reconstruction.

Answers:

"What conditions allow specification to remain possible?"

"How is visibility preserved across iterative refinement?"

"What must be protected so future observers can continue discovering topology?"

Preservation target:

Reconstruction ecology.

Risk:

A stable specification may survive while the ecology that produced it is lost.

One sentence summary:

RSS preserves the conditions under which future specification, reconstruction, and observation remain possible.

---

### Lineage Preservation

Preservation of emergence conditions and stabilization history.

Looks backward.

---

### Reconstructive Sufficiency

Preservation of distinctions required for future reconstruction.

Looks forward.

---

### Provenance

The recoverable history of emergence.

Not necessarily sufficient for reconstruction.

---

## Kairos Vocabulary

### Ecological Marker
