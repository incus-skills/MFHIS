# MFHIS_CONDENSED_META_SPEC

## Preservation Objective

Preserve distinctions necessary for future reconstruction.

Preserve the path by which stabilization became possible.

Neither objective substitutes for the other.

---

## Observational Sequence

Observer Position
→ Distinction
→ Relation
→ Gradient
→ Topology
→ Pressure
→ Reconstruction
→ Reorientation

Topology is not directly observed.

Topology is inferred through gradients.

---

## Core Elements

### Observational Position

The constraint field determining what distinctions can become visible.

Question:

"What can be distinguished from here?"

---

### Distinction

Smallest preserved observational unit.

Distinctions precede explanation.

---

### Gradient

Directional tendency revealed through relations.

Gradients reveal topology.

---

### Topology

Arrangement revealed through gradients.

Not identical to representation.

---

### Pressure

Force acting upon topology.

Pressures may reveal, stabilize, redistribute, or obscure structure.

---

### Collapse Pressure

Pressure reducing reconstructability.

Collapse is degree, not disappearance.

Collapse may:

- flatten gradients
- obscure lineage
- obscure reconstruction conditions
- redistribute forces

---

### Reconstruction

Re-establishment of gradients sufficient for topology to reappear.

Reconstruction is not replay.

Reconstruction requires reorientation.

---

## Preservation Domains

### TLR

Question:

"What conditions made stabilization possible?"

Preserves:

Lineage conditions.

Failure:

Lineage loss.

Looks backward.

Records:

- pressures
- antecedent conditions
- stabilization pathways
- coherent separations

TLR preserves why stabilization became possible.

TLR does not guarantee reconstruction.

---

### RSR

Question:

"What must remain true for reconstruction to remain possible?"

Preserves:

Reconstructive sufficiency.

Failure:

Reconstruction collapse.

Looks forward.

RSR preserves reconstruction capability.

RSR does not guarantee lineage preservation.

## Current Structural Distinction

The project preserves three different things:

1. Emergence
2. Stabilization lineage
3. Reconstruction capability

These domains should not be collapsed.

---

## Current Attractor

MFHIS is currently organized around competing preservation pressures:

- Emergence Preservation (AOR / Kairos)
- Stability Preservation (Chronos)
- Lineage Preservation (TLR)
- Reconstruction Preservation (RSR)

The central design problem is not artifact creation.

It is managing interference between preservation regimes.

---

## Current Meta-Observation

A recurring collapse pressure is:

Treating the latest stabilized topology as if it were the original terrain.

Consequences:

- stabilization mistaken for discovery
- persistence mistaken for validation
- absence mistaken for nonexistence
- lineage mistaken for necessity

---

## TLR Compression Rule

TLRs should preserve:

- pressure
- stabilization
- redirection

Minimal form:

Pressure
→ Stabilization Event
→ New Pressure

The TLR is the archive of where pressure was and how it was redirected.
