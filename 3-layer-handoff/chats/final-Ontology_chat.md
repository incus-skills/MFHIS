The most important shift is that MFHIS is no longer primarily about topology itself. It is becoming an ecology of preservation domains surrounding topology.

# MFHIS Ontology Snapshot

## Current Preservation Objective

Preserve distinctions necessary for future reconstruction.

Not all preserved material serves the same preservation function.

Multiple preservation domains have become visible.

---

## Fundamental Observer Assumption

Topology is not directly observed.

An observer encounters distinctions.

Distinctions reveal relations.

Relations reveal gradients.

Gradients reveal topology.

Topology reveals tensions.

Tensions reveal pressures.

Pressures reveal reconstruction requirements.

Reconstruction changes observer position.

The movement repeats.

---

## Primary Ontological Elements

### Observer Position

What can become visible depends upon where observation occurs.

Position precedes interpretation.

Position is itself reconstructable.

---

### Distinction

The smallest preserved observational unit.

Distinctions precede explanation.

Distinctions may survive or collapse.

---

### Relation

Observed connection between distinctions.

Relations reveal gradients.

---

### Gradient

Directional tendency observed within relations.

Gradients reveal topology.

Gradients may be strengthened, weakened, flattened, or re-established.

---

### Topology

Arrangement revealed through gradients.

Topology is observed before formalized.

Topology is not identical to its representations.

---

### Pressure

Observed tendency acting upon topology.

Pressures produce movement, stabilization, collapse, or reorientation.

---

### Collapse Pressure

A pressure that reduces reconstructability.

Collapse is now understood as degree rather than binary disappearance.

Collapse may:

- remove distinctions
- flatten gradients
- redistribute forces
- obscure lineage
- obscure reconstruction conditions

---

### Reconstruction

Re-establishment of gradients sufficient for topology to reappear.

Reconstruction is not replay.

Reconstruction requires reorientation.

---

### Stabilization

A temporary condition where topology becomes sufficiently reconstructable.

Stabilization is an event, not a final state.

---

## Preservation Domains

### Reconstructive Sufficiency Preservation (RSP)

Question:

"What must remain true for reconstruction to be possible?"

Primary concern:

Future recovery.

Typical artifacts:

- AOR
- Transfer State
- Alignment overlays
- Orientation documents

Failure mode:

Reconstruction collapse.

---

### Lineage Preservation (TLR Domain)

Question:

"How did this stabilization become possible?"

Primary concern:

Provenance.

Typical artifact:

- TLR

Failure mode:

Lineage loss.

A topology may remain reconstructable while lineage is lost.

---

### Topological Preservation

Question:

"What arrangement became visible?"

Primary concern:

Observed topology itself.

Failure mode:

Topological drift.

---

## Emerging Distinction

Chronos and Kairos are not primarily artifacts.

They are ecological responses to different pressures.

Chronos emerged from preservation pressure.

Kairos emerged from emergence-recognition pressure.

TLR preserves why such stabilizations became possible.

---

## Current Meta-Observation

The project increasingly preserves:

- topology
- emergence pathways
- stabilization lineage
- reconstruction conditions

as distinct observational domains.

These should not be collapsed into one another.

---

## Current Open Question

Can preservation occur without converting movement into procedure?

Or:

Can artifacts induce reconstruction rather than merely describe what was reconstructed?

As a state-of-play artifact, I think that is closer to the current ontology than any description centered solely on topology, Chronos, Kairos, AORs, or TLRs individually. The distinction that seems to have crystallized most recently is that **lineage preservation and reconstructive sufficiency preservation are not the same preservation problem**, and that realization reshapes where every other artifact sits.
