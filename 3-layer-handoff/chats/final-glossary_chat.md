Before submitting a final active message, I would separate the vocabulary into three classes:

1. **Core MFHIS terms** (the framework cannot function without them)
2. **MFHIS-derived extensions** (emerged later as the project evolved)
3. **Imported/general terms** (used within MFHIS but not uniquely MFHIS)

That distinction itself helps prevent provenance drift.

# MFHIS Glossary (Current State)

## Core MFHIS Vocabulary

### Observation

The act of permitting distinctions to remain visible before explanation.

Observation precedes interpretation.

---

### Observer Position

The position from which distinctions become visible.

Different positions reveal different topologies.

Position is part of the observation.

---

### Distinction

A separation observed within terrain.

The fundamental unit of observation.

Distinctions precede classification.

---

### Relation

An observed connection between distinctions.

Relations are observed before being named.

---

### Gradient

A directional tendency revealed through relations.

Gradients indicate movement, pressure, tendency, or change.

---

### Topology

The arrangement revealed through gradients.

Not the terrain itself.

A visible organizational structure.

---

### Pressure

A force acting upon topology.

Pressure may reveal or conceal structure.

---

### Collapse Pressure

A pressure capable of flattening distinctions, gradients, or reconstructability.

Collapse is treated as degree rather than absolute disappearance.

---

### Reconstruction

The re-establishment of gradients sufficient for topology to become visible again.

Not replay.

Not duplication.

A reorientation process.

---

### Reorientation

A change in observer position resulting from reconstruction.

Reorientation permits new distinctions to become visible.

---

### Preservation

The maintenance of distinctions required for future reconstruction.

---

### Reconstructability

The ability for topology to become visible again through reconstruction.

The primary preservation criterion.

---

## Secondary MFHIS Vocabulary

### Compliance

The degree to which a topology yields to applied pressure.

Neither positive nor negative.

A property of the observed ecology.

---

### Stabilization

A configuration where gradients remain sufficiently established for topology to persist.

A condition, not a destination.

---

### Stabilization Path

The observed path through which stabilization became possible.

Distinct from the stabilized state itself.

---

### Ecology

The collection of forces, pressures, positions, distinctions, and interactions that generate observable topology.

Usually inferred rather than directly observed.

---

### Terrain

Whatever is being observed.

Topology emerges from terrain but is not identical to terrain.

---

### Visibility

The degree to which distinctions can be observed.

Visibility changes with position.

---

### Observational Position

The stabilized observer position through which topology becomes visible.

---

### Deferred Materialization

The tendency to continue observing after sufficient topology has already emerged.

A recurring collapse pressure.

---

### External Anchoring

Return to practical preservation requirements.

Examples include:

- Session loss
- Context loss
- Hardware failure
- Transfer requirements

---

## Morphological Vocabulary

### Locus

A recurring pattern of tensions, pressures, gradients, or failures.

Observed across multiple contexts.

---

### Morphology

The study of recurring structures across observations.

Concerned with recurrence rather than singular events.

---

### Anti-Locus (Provisional)

A recurring collapse configuration.

Not yet fully stabilized as a formal term.

---

## Preservation Vocabulary

### AOR

After-Observation Record.

Preserves emergence pathways.

Documents what became visible and how.

---

### TLR

Trace Lineage Record.

Preserves lineage conditions.

Documents pressures, antecedents, mutations, and stabilization conditions.

Answers:

"How did this become possible?"

---

### Lineage Preservation

Preservation of emergence conditions and stabilization history.

Looks backward.

---

### Reconstructive Sufficiency

Preservation of distinctions required for future reconstruction.

Looks forward.

---

### Provenance

The recoverable history of emergence.

Not necessarily sufficient for reconstruction.

---

## Chronos Vocabulary

### Chronos

Preservation-oriented instrument.

Concerned with survival, fidelity, collapse, and reconstructability.

Primary question:

"Did structure survive?"

---

### Fidelity

Degree of preservation after transformation.

---

### Compression

Intentional reduction of preserved structure.

Used to test reconstructability.

---

### Collapse Classification

Observed degree and type of flattening during preservation stress.

---

## Kairos Vocabulary

### Kairos

Emergence-oriented instrument.

Concerned with visibility and appearance of structure.

Primary question:

"Did structure emerge?"

---

### Ecological Marker

Observable indicator of emergence.

Originally represented through Marker A–F investigations.

---

### Emergence

The appearance of previously unavailable topology.

## Current Preservation Objective

Preserve distinctions necessary for future reconstruction.

Preserve the path by which stabilization became possible.

Neither objective substitutes for the other.

One thing that becomes visible from the glossary itself is that MFHIS has gradually shifted its preservation target:

**Topology → Reconstructability → Stabilization Path → Lineage Conditions + Reconstructive Sufficiency**

That shift is arguably one of the most important provenance observations of the project so far.
