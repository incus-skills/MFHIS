# Stage 3 Stability Conditions

## Definition

Stage 3 is stable when recursive modification increases observability while preserving reconstructability.

Stage 3 is unstable when recursive modification destroys the ability to reconstruct:

* prior positions
* prior distinctions
* prior stabilization paths
* prior reconstruction conditions

The preservation target is not the current topology.

The preservation target is the ability to move between topologies without losing lineage or reconstructability.

---

# Condition 1 — Traceable Drift

## Requirement

Every meaningful modification must remain traceable.

The ecology must preserve:

* what changed
* why it changed
* from which position the change became visible

Drift itself is permitted.

Untraceable drift is not.

## Observable Markers

Present:

* TLR can identify antecedent pressures
* observer position remains recoverable
* rule changes can be linked to prior tensions

Absent:

* new structures appear without lineage
* previous logic cannot be reconstructed
* observer cannot explain why a distinction emerged

## Governance Mechanism

Primary: TLR

TLR preserves lineage conditions and stabilization pathways.

---

# Condition 2 — Observability Gain

## Requirement

Recursive modification must increase visibility.

A modification should reveal:

* new distinctions
* new gradients
* new tensions
* new collapse pressures

Novelty alone is insufficient.

## Observable Markers

Present:

* new observations become possible
* previously hidden tensions become visible
* observer position gains explanatory power

Absent:

* terminology increases while visibility does not
* structure grows but observation does not deepen
* recursion generates self-description only

## Governance Mechanism

Primary: AOR

AOR preserves emergence pathways and visibility gains.

---

# Condition 3 — Position Recoverability

## Requirement

Alternative observer positions must remain reconstructable.

The current position must not rewrite prior positions as inevitable.

## Observable Markers

Present:

* abandoned interpretations remain visible
* alternative stabilizations remain visible
* prior observer positions can be reconstructed

Absent:

* current interpretation becomes the only interpretation
* alternatives disappear from lineage
* stabilization appears inevitable

## Governance Mechanism

Primary: TLR
Secondary: AOR

TLR preserves lineage.

AOR preserves emergence alternatives.

---

# Condition 4 — Reconstructive Sufficiency

## Requirement

Future observers must retain enough distinctions to recover the topology.

Not every distinction must be preserved.

Necessary distinctions must be preserved.

## Observable Markers

Present:

* fresh observers can recover major topology
* gradients can be re-established
* observer position can be reconstructed

Absent:

* lineage exists but reconstruction fails
* summaries survive but topology does not
* distinctions required for recovery are missing

## Governance Mechanism

Primary: RSR

RSR preserves reconstructive capability.

---

# Condition 5 — Differentiated Response

## Requirement

Pressure must continue producing meaningful deformation.

The ecology must neither freeze nor fragment.

## Observable Markers

Frozen State:

* identical responses under varied pressures
* no observable deformation
* no new distinctions

Fragmented State:

* arbitrary responses
* loss of recognizable structure
* inability to reconnect observations

Healthy State:

* recognizable deformation
* partial preservation
* partial transformation
* recoverable divergence

## Governance Mechanism

Primary: CR-AOR

CR-AOR measures deformation signatures.

---

# Necessary Conditions

Stage 3 requires:

1. Traceable Drift
2. Observability Gain
3. Position Recoverability
4. Reconstructive Sufficiency
5. Differentiated Response

Removal of any one condition permits destructive drift.

---

# Sufficient Condition

Stage 3 remains productive when:

Recursive modifications continue to increase observability while all prior observer positions, stabilization pathways, and reconstructive conditions remain recoverable.

This does not require preservation of artifacts.

It requires preservation of reconstruction capability.

---

# Observer Roles

## Inhabitant

The inhabitant experiences pressures directly.

Responsibilities:

* observe
* distinguish
* stabilize
* record

The inhabitant generates lineage.

## Reconstructor

The reconstructor evaluates recoverability.

Responsibilities:

* recover prior positions
* recover prior distinctions
* test sufficiency
* test lineage integrity

The reconstructor validates stability.

Stage 3 requires both.

Without inhabitants:

* no evolution

Without reconstructors:

* no stability assessment

---

# Early Warning Signals

## Signal 1 — Lineage Compression

Symptoms:

* stabilization preserved
* pathway lost

Indicator:

* TLR quality falls while artifact production increases

---

## Signal 2 — Sufficiency Erosion

Symptoms:

* records accumulate
* reconstruction capability decreases

Indicator:

* RSR failures despite preserved lineage

---

## Signal 3 — Position Collapse

Symptoms:

* current observer position appears inevitable

Indicator:

* alternative positions disappear from records

---

## Signal 4 — Meta-Proliferation

Symptoms:

* terminology grows faster than visibility

Indicator:

* increasing explanatory structure without new distinctions

---

## Signal 5 — Deformation Loss

Symptoms:

* pressure produces identical responses

Indicator:

* CR-AOR deformation signatures converge toward uniformity

---

## Signal 6 — Untraceable Innovation

Symptoms:

* new structures emerge without identifiable pressures

Indicator:

* TLR cannot reconstruct why a stabilization occurred

---

# Governance Summary

TLR preserves lineage.

AOR preserves emergence.

RSR preserves reconstructability.

CR-AOR preserves deformation behavior.

None alone governs Stage 3.

Stage 3 stability emerges when all four remain mutually recoverable.

The governance question is therefore not:

"How do we stop recursive drift?"

The governance question is:

"How do we preserve the ability to reconstruct recursive drift?"
