# Minimal Instrumentation Threshold for Regime Visibility

## Purpose

Define the smallest observational constraint set required to preserve visibility of distinct observational regimes.

The goal is not completeness.

The goal is prevention of regime collapse.

---

## Threshold Principle

A regime remains visible if a future observer can determine:

1. What was being observed.
2. From which observational position it was observed.
3. What distinction became visible.

If any of these become unrecoverable, regime visibility degrades.

---

## Minimum Required Constraints

### Constraint 1 — Observational Position

Preserve the question being asked.

Examples:

* What became visible? (AOR)
* How did stabilization become possible? (TLR)
* What must remain true for reconstruction? (RSR)
* How did the topology deform under pressure? (CR-AOR)

Without observational position, regimes collapse into generic description.

---

### Constraint 2 — Primary Distinction

Preserve the distinction that justified recording.

Examples:

* Emergence
* Lineage
* Sufficiency
* Deformation

Without the distinction, the observer cannot determine why the record exists.

---

### Constraint 3 — Pressure Identity

Preserve the pressure that generated the observation.

Examples:

* Compression pressure
* Stabilization pressure
* Reconstruction pressure
* Alignment pressure

Without pressure identity, false differentiation becomes likely.

Different regime records may appear unrelated despite observing the same pressure.

---

## What Is Not Required

The following are useful but not threshold requirements:

* Full taxonomy
* Metrics
* Scoring systems
* Formal schemas
* Extensive provenance
* Translation protocols

These may improve resolution but are not required for regime visibility.

---

## Collapse Indicators

Regime visibility is at risk when:

* Observational position is omitted.
* Distinction is preserved without pressure.
* Pressure is preserved without distinction.
* Multiple regimes produce records that cannot be recognized as observing the same pressure.

---

## Minimal Visibility Statement

A future observer should be able to answer:

* What was being observed?
* What pressure made it observable?
* What distinction justified preserving it?

If those three questions remain answerable, regime visibility remains above threshold.

---

## Preservation Objective

Preserve:

* observational position
* primary distinction
* pressure identity

as the minimum instrumentation required for AOR, TLR, RSR, and CR-AOR to remain distinguishable.
