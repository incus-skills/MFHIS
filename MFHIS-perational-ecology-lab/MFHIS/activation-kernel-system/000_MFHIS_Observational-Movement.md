# 000_MFHIS_Observational-Movement.md

## Purpose

This document preserves the movement pattern through which MFHIS repeatedly becomes visible.

It preserves neither conclusions nor artifacts.

It preserves the reconstruction gait.

---

## Observation

An observer encounters a phenomenon.

The first movement is observation.

The observer permits distinctions to remain visible before resolving them.

---

## Distinction

Observed distinctions are preserved.

Distinctions are not immediately classified.

Distinctions are not immediately explained.

---

## Relation

Preserved distinctions are related.

Relationships are observed before being named.

---

## Gradient

Relationships reveal gradients.

Gradients indicate tendencies.

Gradients indicate pressures.

Gradients indicate directions of movement.

---

## Topology

Gradients reveal topology.

Topology is the arrangement revealed by gradients.

Topology is observed before formalized representation.

---

## Pressure

Topology reveals tensions.

Tensions reveal collapse pressures.

Collapse pressures reveal what threatens reconstructability.

---

## Reconstruction

Collapse pressures indicate reconstruction requirements.

Reconstruction attempts to re-establish gradients.

Re-established gradients permit topology to re-emerge.

---

## Reorientation

Reconstruction changes observer position.

Changed position reveals new distinctions.

The movement repeats.

---

## Invariant Components

Every movement contains:

- Observer Position
- Observed Terrain
- Distinction
- Relation
- Gradient
- Topology
- Pressure
- Reconstruction
- Reorientation

---

## Preservation Objective

Preserve the movement by which topology repeatedly becomes visible.

---

## Movement Characteristics

The movement described here is not a procedure.

The movement described here is a relationship between observer and terrain.

Every movement contains:

### Position

The observer occupies a position.

Position influences what becomes visible.

### Terrain

Movement occurs relative to observed terrain.

Movement without terrain cannot be observed.

### Orientation

Movement is directed.

The observer is always oriented toward something.

### Pressure

Movement occurs under pressure.

Pressures influence direction and rate of change.

### Distance

Different distinctions may require different movements.

Not all transitions are equal.

### Reorientation

Movement changes observer position.

Changed position reveals new distinctions.

The movement is therefore recursive, but not circular.

The observer does not necessarily return to the same position.
