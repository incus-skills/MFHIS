# MFHIS Provenance Note: Recursion-Level Shifts

Observation:

The name "MFHIS" remained comparatively stable while the referent repeatedly changed.

The shifts were not primarily conceptual.

They were pressure-induced.

The system repeatedly moved to a higher observational position because lower positions ceased to adequately preserve reconstructability.

---

Early Pressure

Initial pressure was explanatory insufficiency.

Structures were visible.

Relationships were visible.

The mechanism by which visibility itself appeared was not.

What became visible:

- Relationships between observations.
- Reconstructive patterns.

What remained largely invisible:

- Observer position.
- Visibility conditions.

MFHIS at this stage tended to appear as a framework.

---

Subsequent Pressure

Framework descriptions repeatedly collapsed into interpretation.

Observers could reconstruct conclusions.

Observers could not reliably reconstruct how conclusions became possible.

Pressure:

- Loss of emergence pathway.
- Premature synthesis.
- Framework reification.

What became visible:

- Reconstruction as distinct from explanation.
- Position as a prerequisite for interpretation.

What became less visible:

- The apparent solidity of the framework itself.

MFHIS increasingly appeared as an observational discipline rather than a framework.

---

Further Pressure

Reconstruction itself proved insufficient.

Artifacts could survive.

The movement that regenerated artifacts could still be lost.

Pressure:

- Artifact preservation replacing movement preservation.
- Stable outputs obscuring reconstructive process.

What became visible:

- Gradients.
- Topology.
- Collapse pressures.
- Reorientation.

What became less visible:

- Artifacts as preservation targets.

MFHIS increasingly appeared as a movement through topology.

---

Recent Pressure

Topology preservation revealed another gap.

A future observer might reconstruct topology while losing:

- Why stabilization occurred.
- What pressures made stabilization necessary.
- What alternatives existed.

Pressure:

- Provenance loss.
- Stabilization-path loss.

What became visible:

- Lineage preservation.
- Stabilization pathways.
- Distinction between lineage and reconstructive sufficiency.

This pressure contributed to emergence of TLR.

---

Character of the Shifts

The shifts do not resemble:

Framework
→ Better Framework
→ Better Framework

They more closely resemble:

Explanation
→ Reconstruction
→ Observational Position
→ Topology
→ Movement Through Topology
→ Conditions of Stabilization
→ Conditions of Future Reconstruction

At each shift:

A previously sufficient description became incapable of preserving something now recognized as necessary.

The recursion increase was therefore not driven by abstraction for its own sake.

It was driven by preservation failure.

---

Visibility Pattern

Each recursion-level increase revealed:

- New pressures.
- New dependencies.
- New preservation targets.

Each increase simultaneously obscured:

- The apparent centrality of previous preservation targets.

The framework did not disappear.

The framework became local.

Topology did not disappear.

Topology became local.

Artifacts did not disappear.

Artifacts became local.

---

Stability of the Name

The stability of the term "MFHIS" was largely retrospective.

At each stage the name referred to "the thing currently necessary to preserve reconstructability."

Only after multiple shifts does it appear that a single object was being developed.

An equally valid interpretation is that a succession of preservation targets became visible under the same name.

The continuity was carried less by definitions and more by recurring pressures.
