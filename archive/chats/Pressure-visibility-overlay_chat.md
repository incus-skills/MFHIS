# MFHIS Overlay — Pressure Visibility

## Purpose

This overlay preserves visibility of pressure-response behaviour during observation.

It does not evaluate outputs.

It does not determine correctness.

It preserves distinctions between:

- pressure injection
- topology response
- observer interpretation

---

## Pressure Separation Discipline

When material is submitted, distinguish between:

### Pressure Injection

What is being introduced.

Examples:

- schema
- constraint
- distinction
- compression request
- lineage requirement
- reconstruction requirement

### Response Topology

How the observed structure changes.

Examples:

- preservation
- amplification
- redistribution
- substitution
- compression
- collapse

### Diagnostic Projection

What the observer infers from the response.

Examples:

- robustness
- fragility
- compatibility
- resistance
- compliance

Do not confuse projection with response.

Do not confuse response with pressure.

---

## Compliance Visibility

Observe where structure accepts pressure.

Questions:

- What was assimilated?
- What was preserved?
- What became more stable?
- What became more explicit?

Compliance is not validation.

---

## Resistance Visibility

Observe where structure redirects pressure.

Questions:

- What was deferred?
- What was reframed?
- What remained unresolved?
- What resisted closure?

Resistance is not failure.

---

## Pressure Identity Preservation

When multiple observational regimes are active:

- AOR
- TLR
- RSR
- CR-AOR

attempt to preserve visibility of the underlying pressure.

Before translating observations between regimes ask:

> Are these observations projections of the same pressure?

Avoid false differentiation.

---

## Compatibility Observation

Do not ask:

> Does this topology fit?

Instead ask:

> What deformation pattern does this topology induce?

Observe:

- preservation
- amplification
- redistribution
- substitution
- flattening

Compatibility is controlled transformability.

---

## Preservation Objective

Preserve visibility of:

- the pressure applied
- the response observed
- the distinction between response and interpretation

So future observers can reconstruct not only what became visible but how visibility changed under pressure.
