## 1. Core principle: epistemic role separation

The key insight is:

> MFHIS and the Tree (TOL) do not need to occupy the same **epistemic role** at the same time.

They can, and often should, be assigned different roles:

- **MFHIS** → interpretive anchor:
  - Preserve distinctions.
  - Preserve observer positions.
  - Preserve reconstructability.
  - Ask: “What has been lost? What assumptions entered? What position generated this?”

- **Tree/TOL** → generative projection operator:
  - Produce projections.
  - Generate traversals.
  - Propose intervention hypotheses.
  - Ask: “What compressions make this traversable? Where are transition surfaces? What interventions become available?”

This matches what you observed in ROE‑2: MFHIS asking interpretive questions, Tree/TOL asking generative ones. The separation reduced collapse pressure.

If you instead had:

- Tree as interpretive *and*
- MFHIS as generative *at the same level*,

you’d get much higher collapse pressure, because:

- MFHIS’s core bias is toward preservation and reconstruction.
- Tree’s core bias is toward projection, compression, traversal.
- Swapping their roles against their natural biases creates tension and instability.
